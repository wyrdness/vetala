## Overview

The vetala (Sanskrit: वेताल, vetāla) is a paranormal entity in Hindu mythology described as a disembodied spirit that uses reanimated corpses as vehicles. Vetalas are said to inhabit charnel grounds and burning ghats, where they may be approached or captured by tantric practitioners. They possess supernatural knowledge — including knowledge of the past, present, and future — and are most famous in the literary tradition through the Vetala Panchavimshati ('Twenty-Five Tales of the Vetala'), a frame-tale cycle preserved within Somadeva's eleventh-century Sanskrit Kathasaritsagara, in which King Vikramaditya repeatedly carries a vetala-possessed corpse from a cremation ground while the spirit poses riddles. In regional traditions, especially of the Konkan coast, Vetoba/Betal is venerated as a guardian deity and consort of the goddess Shantadurga, with established temples. Vetalas are typically distinguished from other Indian undead such as pretas (hungry ghosts) and bhutas (ghosts of the unsettled dead) by their specific association with corpses and their oracular function.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: Sanskrit वेताल (vetāla); the etymology is uncertain but the term is attested in classical Sanskrit literature and tantric texts.
- **Meaning**: A class of spirit that animates corpses; sometimes glossed simply as 'ghoul' or 'corpse-spirit' in English translations.
- **First use**: Classical Sanskrit; the Vetala Panchavimshati cycle is preserved in Somadeva's Kathasaritsagara, compiled in the 11th century CE.

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Tantric corpse-spirit** _(Tantric and folk Hindu traditions)_

Within Hindu and tantric tradition, the vetala is understood as a real spirit-class that may be propitiated, bound, or commanded by ritual specialists, particularly at cremation grounds.

### Skeptical

**Literary motif and folk explanation of corpse phenomena** _(Comparative folklore scholarship)_

Comparative folklorists treat the vetala as a literary and folk motif that gives narrative form to anxieties about the dead, the cremation ground, and bodily decay rather than as a discrete external entity.
